# Production at 450K orders/s: the plan from the 2026-11-09/10 campaign

Written 2026-11-10 1:10 am ET (trade-history-tiers lane) from RI34 §1b. The campaign showed the binary order path,
the capture stream and the history store sustaining 464K orders/s for an hour on a rig that differs from production
in six ways. This plan takes each difference to a production decision, with a recommendation, the work it implies,
and what proves it. Nothing here is a production write; every production change still needs yaakov's yes on the
change itself. Sequence at the end.

**Decided 2026-11-10 ~9:00 am ET by yaakov ("Yes, all five"): 1 B (three servers), 2 c4d-standard-16 with the 520K
re-test deciding the timeouts, 4 the router by `account mod 8`, 5 the same 450K target for FIX, 6 standing capacity.**
Recorded as ADR-103; the NATS cluster and the members' shape are rendered (FR-PRG-41, FR-PRG-05, FR-DRS-44), RI35 and
RI36 run in their own sessions. Nothing is applied to production yet.

## 1. The capture stream's storage and durability

**What the rig did.** The hour ran on memory-backed streams (8 × 8 GB, about two minutes of feed). File-backed
streams saturated their volume twice: a pd-balanced 416 GB at 298 MB/s (300K), a Hyperdisk Balanced provisioned at
1,200 MB/s at 684–724 MB/s once `max_bytes` deletions joined the writes (400K), each time stalling a partition's
JetStream API for seconds and leaving the leader's publishes unacked. s2 compression cut the steady write to
311 MB/s but not the churn.

**What production needs.** The stream is the readers' outage window: downstream-at-400k.md §7.5 sizes it to two
hours so a reader can be down that long and lose nothing. A memory stream gives up that window and empties on a
server restart. One server is also the only server: no failover.

**Recommendation: a three-server NATS cluster with file-backed, s2-compressed, R1 streams placed by partition
(the options paper's B), each server on a c4d-standard-16 with its own Hyperdisk Balanced volume.** Each server
then writes a third of the bytes (about 100–150 MB/s compressed at 450K, well under one volume's ceiling even with
retention churn), the stream keeps its two-hour window, and a server loss loses only its partitions' unreplayed
tail, which FR-DRS-38's catch-up republishes. R3 replication is not recommended: three times the ingest for a
window the Aeron log already covers. Memory storage stays a rig setting (`PROJECTION_FEED_STREAM_STORAGE=memory`).

Not recommended: one bigger disk under one server. We do not know the per-VM Hyperdisk ceiling that stopped us at
~700 MB/s, a single server stays a single point of failure, and retention churn still lands on one device.

**Work.** A `nats` StatefulSet of three with routes and server tags; stream placement by tag in `JetStreamFeed`
(`PROJECTION_FEED_STREAM_PLACEMENT`, one tag per partition group); the bridges and readers connect to the cluster
(the client library follows the stream's server); PVC per server; `max_pending` 256 MB per connection; ADR for the
placement and the loss model; the subject map. Proof: FR-DRS-34's cluster bench already exists (1.25M messages/s
on three servers); repeat it on the production render with batching, then the one-hour run below.

**Decision for yaakov:** B (three servers) as recommended, or a single server on a larger provisioned volume.

## 2. The members' machine and the cluster's timeouts

**What the rig did.** On production's c4d-standard-8 with a 16 Gi container the leader held 400K at 6.6 of 7 cores.
On c4d-standard-16 with the same 16 Gi it accepted 510–527K for 17 minutes and then flapped: elections, `failed to
join catchup log`, one member dead on `Media Driver timeout` (its Aeron driver's heartbeat missed for 10 s). The
archive's page cache and the /dev/shm term buffers count against the container's memory; at 50–65 MB/s of log the
16 Gi cgroup lives in reclaim, and a stall past the 200 ms heartbeat timeout is an election. At 40 Gi and
1000/500 ms timeouts the hour ran without one.

**Recommendation: members on c4d-standard-16 with a 15-CPU / 40 Gi container.** On the timeouts, do not simply
keep 1000/500 ms: NFR-AC03 bought sub-second failover detection with 200/100 ms, and the flapping may have been the
memory alone. Re-run the 520K step at 40 Gi with 200/100 ms; keep 200/100 if it holds, otherwise the smallest
timeouts that hold (try 500/250 before 1000/500), and record the measured failover time at the chosen setting.

**Work.** The engine pool's machine type and the StatefulSet's resources in the production render; the timeout env
if it changes; FR/NFR text in the cluster spec. Proof: the 520K step without an election, then a failover at rate
(the e1108 failover proof) at the chosen timeouts.

**Decision for yaakov:** the machine class (c4d-standard-16 recommended) and whether failover detection may be
slower than today's sub-second if the re-test needs it.

## 3. The acceptors: one order in flight per session (RI35)

**What the rig did.** The binary acceptor runs one thread per TCP connection and blocks in `dispatch` until the
committed ack is back (~0.5–1 ms), so a session carries one order at a time: 128 sessions gave ~300K whatever the
gateway count, 512 gave 400K+. The FIX acceptor has the same shape: QuickFIX/J's `fromApp` runs on one dispatcher
thread per session and calls `submitOrder` synchronously.

**Recommendation: pipeline both acceptors.** Submit without waiting (`submitPipelined` already builds a
`PendingOrder` with a future; expose an asynchronous variant), bound the in-flight orders per connection (4,096,
beyond which the connection thread stops reading and TCP back-pressures the client), and write each ack as its
future completes from one writer per connection. Binary acks carry the clOrdId, and the load driver already
matches by it, so acks may complete out of order; the spec must say so. FIX: the ExecutionReport is sent from the
completion on the session (QuickFIX/J's `Session.sendToTarget` is thread-safe); sequencing semantics for
`FIX_SEQUENCED` are unchanged because the order of submission to the cluster is the order of arrival.

**Work.** `BinaryGatewayAcceptor` and `FixGatewayAcceptor` in the order-matcher override, an FR for the acceptor's
pipelining and ack ordering, `BinaryGatewayAcceptorTest` with a fake submitter that completes late, the kind
binary proof with 16 sessions reaching the 512-session rate, and `/latency`'s `queue` segment staying flat.
Then the gateway count is set by CPU, not by sessions: measure one pipelined gateway's orders/s per core on kind
before choosing the production count (today's render: 2, up to 18; the hour used 24).

**No decision needed** beyond approving the FR's ack-ordering statement.

## 4. The read model (RI36, FR-DRS-43)

**What the rig did.** trade-processor fetched 1,000 messages per partition, sized for one-record messages; at the
batched wire that was ~12 MB a fetch over 16 feeds: NATS cut it as a slow consumer 351 times in five minutes, it ran
4–5M messages behind, was OOM-killed three times at 24 Gi, and then fail-closed on `RETENTION_GAP` against the
two-minute memory stream. FR-DRS-43 (the fetch sized by bytes) is in; on the rig it cut the cuts to one in eight
minutes and G3 passed in a six-minute run.

**Recommendation: ship FR-DRS-43 to production as is, and shard the read model by partition across replicas.**
The stream is already partitioned by `account mod 8`; positions and orders never cross a partition. Run N
trade-processor replicas (start with 4, two partitions each; `PROJECTION_FEED_PARTITIONS_OWNED` per replica), each
with its own snapshot cursors. The SQL projection needs no routing (MariaDB is shared). The in-memory order view's
HTTP reads (`/v2/projections/...`, `/orders`) need a router: the smallest honest one is the gateway's existing
`order-matcher` Service fronted by a thin router that forwards by `account mod 8` (or the console asks every
replica and merges, which is simpler and fine at N = 4). Also keep the per-record JSON parse in view: at 450K the
reader spends most of its cores there; the compact wire (the options paper's F) is the next cut if replicas are not
enough.

**Work.** Replica ownership env and start-up guard (a partition owned twice is a refusal), the router, the
deployment with N replicas, FR text, the kind proof that every partition is read exactly once across replicas,
and the hour below with G3 on every replica. Also: the run's far end takes G3 after the read model drains and does
not gate the hour on it.

**Decision for yaakov:** the router (recommended: forward by `account mod 8`) versus the console merging N answers.

## 5. FIX at rate

**What the rig did.** Nothing: FIX carried no load and its fill feed was off, because the fill feed's 18 consumers
each read every order event (an 18× fan-out) and the campaign was about the order path.

**Recommendation: after RI35, run the FIX rehearsal load (600 sessions, `fix-rehearsal-load.mjs`) at rate with the
fill feed on, with the fill feed changed to per-account subjects first (the options paper's E1: a gateway subscribes
only to the accounts of its sessions).** Without E1 every gateway reads the whole order feed and the readers' bytes
multiply by the gateway count.

**Work.** E1 in `ClusterGatewayMain`'s fill feed (subscribe `/orders.<p>` for the partitions its sessions' accounts
fall in; the subject map already partitions by account), an FR, the FIX load run at 100K then 300K with the hour's
checks. FIX exactly-once stays off (lane rule).

**Decision for yaakov:** whether the FIX rate target is the same 450K or a lower figure that matches real FIX
session counts.

## 6. The full service set, limits, and cost

**What the rig did.** Only the pipeline's services ran (members, gateways, NATS, trade-processor, position-service,
MariaDB, ClickHouse, history-feed); the EOD risk extract, the risk engine, price-publisher, reference-data,
trade-service, the account and people services, the web front ends and observability were not deployed. The risk
policy's `maxPositionQuantity` was raised because the load's eight accounts accumulate positions no real account
would; production's limits stay.

**Recommendation.** The acceptance run is the production render (`prepare.py`, the whole service set), not the
p4k cut: one hour at 450K with FIX on, the full set deployed, observability scraping, and the EOD extract run at the
end against the resulting positions. Spread the load over enough accounts that no account meets a real limit.

**Cost.** The hour's shape ran at about $16/h (24 gateways c4d-8, 3 × c4d-16 members, c3-44 NATS, 2 × c4d-16
readers, 2 load nodes) against production's ~$3–4/h. With RI35 the gateway count falls to what CPU needs; with B
the NATS machine becomes 3 × c4d-16 (~$2.3/h). Expect production at this capacity at roughly $8–10/h on demand;
decide whether that is the standing shape or a shape the rig scales to for campaigns.

**Decision for yaakov:** standing capacity at ~$8–10/h, or scale-up on demand.

## Sequence

1. **Now, no decision needed:** RI35 binary acceptor pipelining (kind proof); FR-DRS-43 is already in; RI36's
   replica ownership and the partition-exactly-once proof on kind.
2. **On decisions 1 and 2:** the three-server NATS render + ADR; members' machine/memory/timeouts in the render;
   the 520K re-test at 40 Gi with 200/100 ms.
3. **On decision 4:** the read-model router; N replicas in the render.
4. RI35 for FIX; E1; the FIX load at rate.
5. **Acceptance:** the production render, full service set, one hour at 450K with FIX on, G3 on every replica,
   G3h, the EOD extract at the end. Then the production rollout, change by change, each with yaakov's yes.

## Also in this plan (from the hour)

- A memory-backed stream's retention must cover the read model's longest restart; with file-backed streams at two
  hours this is moot, and the spec says so (FR-DRS-42).
- G3h's own ClickHouse query exceeds the 21 GiB query memory at 1.4B rows; make it the count/bounds/modular-sum
  form the campaign used by hand.
- The bridges' ack gap (FR-DRS-33): resend a publish past a receipt timeout inside a leader's life. The hour had
  none because the stream never stalled; the file-backed takes lost 1.9M and 25M sides to it.
